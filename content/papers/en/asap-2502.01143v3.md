# ASAP: Aligning Training Simulation to Hardware with Residual Actions

[中文版](../asap-2502.01143v3.md)

Sources: [arXiv:2502.01143v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01143v3) · [version-pinned official code](https://github.com/LeCAR-Lab/ASAP/tree/df5320cc47dd8cad97961bdfabfe402dd62ad999)

Review scope: the complete 18-page paper, ablations, appendix, and the official code at the pinned commit.

> In one sentence: ASAP learns an action-space residual that makes replayed simulation transitions resemble recorded hardware, freezes that residual while fine-tuning the main policy, and deploys only the fine-tuned main policy.

Key terms are dynamics alignment (动力学对齐), system identification (SysID，系统辨识), action residual or delta action (动作残差), open-loop replay (开环回放), compounding error (累积误差), out-of-distribution motion (OOD，分布外动作), frozen residual model (冻结残差模型), and operational design domain (ODD，操作设计域).

## Engineering problem

The sim-to-real gap includes mass, friction, and damping, but also actuator inner loops, filtered sensing, communication delay, backlash, compliance, and contact-solver differences. Conventional SysID calibrates named physical parameters and remains interpretable, yet may miss hidden parts of the chain. Learning arbitrary state dynamics can bypass useful simulator integration and contact structure.

ASAP asks a narrower question for each hardware trajectory: what correction added to the recorded action would make the simulator's next state closer to the real next state? It is analogous to learning a small control-surface bias rather than rebuilding an entire wind tunnel.

This requires hardware data from the dynamic region one hopes to align. The paper reports motor overheating and damage to two G1 robots during collection. Those are not incidental implementation notes; they define a central cost and safety boundary of data-driven alignment.

## Core insight

The residual is a training-time simulator adapter, not a second controller deployed beside the actor. A main policy is fine-tuned while the residual is frozen; the residual is removed on hardware. This keeps the deployment graph simple and forces the main policy to absorb behavior learned in the aligned environment.

Better replay and better closed-loop policy must be tested separately. Table III evaluates open-loop prediction; Tables IV–V evaluate policies fine-tuned in the resulting simulator. A map that better matches landmarks does not automatically produce a better route planner.

The small-residual penalty preserves calibration semantics. An unrestricted residual could rewrite each recorded action into a new controller. Norm regularization asks the simulator to explain as much as possible and uses correction only where recorded transitions disagree.

## Method: input → processing → output

A 23-DoF G1 tracking policy is first trained in Isaac Gym from retargeted human motion and deployed to collect state-action trajectories. Replaying recorded actions in simulation produces next-state error. A residual policy observes current simulated state and recorded action, outputs `Δa`, and advances the simulator with `a_real + Δa`; reward balances matching the real next state against small correction magnitude.

The residual is then frozen and used as aligned training dynamics for fine-tuning the main policy. Deployment runs only the fine-tuned main actor. Simulation experiments study full 23-joint residuals, but hardware constraints allow only a four-ankle-joint residual trained from about 100 clips rather than the estimated 400+ clips for full coverage.

Because timestamp mismatch can masquerade as actuator dynamics, every state, command, PD target, sensor sample, and MoCap pose needs a common time base before residual training.

## Strongest experiment

Tables III and IV create a useful causal chain: replay becomes more accurate, then a policy fine-tuned under that alignment remains successful with lower error in two other simulators. Table IV reports 100% success on the selected difficult motions across IsaacSim and Genesis, with Genesis global error 129 mm versus 175 for Vanilla, 186 for SysID, and 190 for DeltaDynamics.

Open-loop one-second accuracy and long closed-loop contact are different scales. Reproduction must plot error growth at multiple horizons and stratify by in-distribution/OOD motion, load, temperature, and actuator state.

## Paper-to-code mapping

- `DeltaA_OpenLoop._compute_torques` and `get_open_loop_action_at_current_timestep` combine learned correction with recorded action before PD torque computation.
- `DeltaA_OpenLoop._reward_minimal_action_norm` and related rewards implement the small-correction regularizer and its ablations.
- `DeltaA_ClosedLoop._compute_torques` and `get_closed_loop_action_at_current_timestep` combine main-policy and frozen residual actions in aligned fine-tuning and support scaling/mixed-environment variants.
- `PPODeltaA._rollout_step` produces residual and loaded main-policy actions in the delta environment.

## Limitations and safety boundary

The authors explicitly report hardware damage and overheating, MoCap dependence, the need for 400+ clips for an estimated full 23-DoF hardware residual, and their practical restriction to 100 clips and four ankle joints. A residual can reproduce a hardware failure without identifying real physical parameters and may absorb sensing or control-stack bias.

Independent limits include firmware, gains, battery, load, temperature, wear, sensor calibration, and MoCap alignment as part of the residual model version. Updating any one can reverse or overcompensate the learned correction. Dangerous or intentionally avoided motions are underrepresented, creating lowest data density exactly where risk is highest.

This method requires a damage budget, not only a training budget. High-dynamic collection must use energy tiers, current/temperature stop thresholds, cooldowns, fall protection, inspection, independent emergency stop, and explicit records of rejected or down-scaled trials.

## Bounded engineering takeaway

Treat delta action as a versioned calibration layer with a declared ODD. Test whether it reproduces known hardware failures on held-out trajectories, not only whether average success trajectories become closer. Compare original simulation, SysID, delta dynamics, uniform action noise, norm-matched fixed noise, and aligned fine-tuning under equal data and training budgets.

## Reproduction and acceptance checklist

Establish time synchronization first. Evaluate one step, 0.25 s, 0.5 s, and 1.0 s errors by root pose, local joints, velocity, contact events, and residual norm. Map data coverage by motion family, joint state, torque, temperature, voltage, and payload. Freeze residual weights, normalization, commit, firmware, and control stack throughout main-policy fine-tuning, with locked replay trajectories as regression checks. Preserve damaged, overheated, stopped, and intentionally unexecuted trials as negative evidence rather than silently removing them.
