# APEX: Learning Common Bipedal Gaits by Parameterizing Contact Rhythm

[中文版](../apex-2011.01387.md)

Sources: [arXiv:2011.01387](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.01387) · [pinned official code](https://github.com/osudrl/apex/tree/82c44af2b5d7bffe884fd2f69856bb1b3c9948e0)

Review scope: the complete seven-page paper and reward appendix, plus official Cassie phase, clock-reward, LSTM-policy, and environment code. The catalog's former `2010.01909` identifier referred to an unrelated paper and is corrected to `2011.01387`.

> In one sentence: APEX replaces joint reference trajectories with phase offsets, duty factors, and foot-force/velocity preferences, learning Cassie standing, walking, running, hopping, galloping, and skipping with hardware transfer; the prior is powerful for periodic gait, not arbitrary aperiodic motion.

Key terms include periodic reward composition (周期奖励组合), gait phase (步态相位), swing phase (摆动期), stance phase (支撑期), phase offset (相位偏移), duty factor (占空比), ground reaction force (地面反作用力), recurrent policy (循环策略), and sim-to-real (仿真到现实).

## Engineering problem

Reference trajectories specify gait precisely but restrict adaptation to one curve. A generic “move forward and do not fall” reward is under-specified and may produce dragging, hopping, or simulation exploits. Multi-gait learning needs contact timing without fixing every joint trajectory.

Common bipedal gaits differ mainly by rhythm: walking alternates feet with double support, running introduces flight, hopping synchronizes feet, galloping offsets them, and skipping needs four phases. This resembles describing music by meter and entries rather than every note; dynamics fills in joint detail.

Phase boundaries must be smooth, and phase, duty factor, and commands must be visible to the policy. Otherwise reward discontinuity and hidden nonstationarity corrupt learning.

## Core insight

Cycle time is normalized to φ∈[0,1). Each reward component multiplies a phase coefficient, a random interval indicator, and a measured quantity. Wrapped Von Mises distributions define interval boundaries, while training uses the expected indicator, producing a smooth periodic reward.

Foot force and velocity form a minimal behavior description. Swing penalizes force and permits velocity; stance penalizes velocity and permits force. Left and right feet share the template with offsets. Hop synchronizes, walk/run offset by half a cycle, gallop uses another offset, and skip combines four phases.

Phase parameters also enter policy observation. A two-layer 128-unit LSTM at 40 Hz outputs ten joint PD targets and ten P/D gain pairs, thirty values total, while low-level PD runs at 2 kHz. Recurrent state helps infer partially observed contact dynamics.

## Method: input → processing → output

State includes estimated pose and velocity, desired forward/lateral speed, phase ratios, and sine/cosine clock inputs. Reward combines periodic foot force/velocity with orientation, command tracking, action difference, torque, and smoothness. A standing region suppresses hopping at zero command.

PPO uses 32 trajectories of 300 steps, learning rate `1e-4`, a 50k replay capacity, four epochs, roughly 150 million samples, and 24–36 hours per policy. Randomization spans damping 0.3–4.0×, mass 0.5–1.5×, friction 0.35–1.1, slope about ±0.03 rad, and encoder offset ±0.05.

Single-gait and generic policies are trained. Continuously varying every offset and ratio causes fused or asymmetric behavior, so transition penalties and structure are needed for a generic two-beat controller. Four-phase skipping remains special.

## Strongest experiment

The strongest evidence is one framework producing and transferring hopping, walking, running, galloping, and skipping on Cassie, including multi-gait transitions and qualitative curb, crowned-road, turf, and blind five-stair demonstrations.

Hardware evidence remains mostly qualitative. There are no repeated success rates, fall counts, energy statistics, or seed intervals, so the result establishes feasibility rather than unconditional reliability.

## Paper-to-code mapping

At commit `82c44af2b5d7bffe884fd2f69856bb1b3c9948e0`, `cassie/phase_function.py::create_phase_reward` builds swing and stance intervals and uses `PchipInterpolator` over three cycles for continuous force/velocity coefficients.

`cassie/rewards/clock_rewards.py::clock_reward` combines coefficients with force, speed, and posture. `rl/policies/actor.py::LSTM_Actor` and `Gaussian_LSTM_Actor` implement recurrent policies. Many historical environment variants are present, so reproduction must pin the exact entry configuration.

## Limitations and safety boundary

The authors explicitly limit the framework to periodic behavior; transfer to quadrupeds, other biped morphology, and aperiodic one-shot skills is unresolved. Continuous parameter changes can create fusion and asymmetry.

Independent limitations include 150M-sample cost, hidden-state mismatch after reset or packet loss, dependence on Cassie compliance and a 2 kHz inner loop, and qualitative terrain evidence without repetition.

A phase reward is not a contact constraint. Hardware still needs joint, torque, speed, collision, posture, transition, estimator-health, and emergency-stop guards. Abrupt gait-parameter changes should be rate-limited or accepted only at safe phases.

## Bounded engineering takeaway

The reusable idea is to specify contact rhythm and let policy dynamics invent joint trajectories. Start from force, contact, and speed templates and add only necessary task terms.

Getting up, landing, recovery, and manipulation are event-driven. They require state machines, event variables, or skill transitions rather than forcing everything into a clock.

## Reproduction and acceptance checklist

Pin paper ID, commit, model, estimator, PPO, and randomization. Unit-test continuity at φ=0/1 and verify offsets, duty factors, and four-phase windows. Report command tracking, contact, GRF, slip, flight/double support, energy, falls, and transitions across seeds.

Sweep every randomization dimension and test outside-range degradation. Stage hardware through standing, one gait, slow transitions, and surfaces, logging torque, impact, estimator reset, and temperature. Rate-limit commands and fall back on abnormal force, posture, or communication.

> **Engineering judgment:** periodic reward gives the policy a beat rather than a script; the beat organizes gait but cannot replace contact safety or aperiodic planning.
