# HoST: Smooth Get-Up Control from Diverse Fallen Postures

[中文版](../host-2502.08378v2.md)

Sources: [arXiv:2502.08378v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08378v2) · [version-pinned official code](https://github.com/OpenRobotLab/HoST/tree/70bb580949a336a920833700e4b5dc3bf7fe87ce)

Review scope: the complete 13-page paper, limitations, appendix, and official code at the pinned commit.

> In one sentence: HoST separates task, style, regularization, and post-stand objectives with multiple critics, then gradually removes vertical assistance and shrinks action scale so G1 can discover and refine get-up behavior without demonstrations.

Key terms are get-up control (起身控制), multi-critic learning (多评论家学习), exploration-assistance force (探索辅助力), action-rescaling curriculum (动作缩放课程), L2C2 regularization (L2 局部平滑正则), second-order action difference (二阶动作差分), domain randomization (域随机化), and implicit contact estimation (隐式接触估计).

## Engineering problem

Locomotion policies start near a narrow upright manifold. Get-up starts with trunk, arms, knees, and feet in varied contact. Early random actions almost never lift head and pelvis enough to reveal useful task reward; rare successes may exploit violent bounce, foot sliding, or high-frequency oscillation.

One scalar critic must fit sparse task progress and dense smoothness, energy, style, and final-standing terms of different scale and phase. Two trajectories can have the same total return but entirely different failure causes. Multiple critics retain those structures while still producing one actor.

Environment geometry matters. A hand support that works on flat ground may collide near a wall, slope, or platform. HoST trains across these environments, but the hardware actor remains proprioceptive and cannot inspect clearance visually.

## Core insight

Multiple critics do not mean multiple policies. Four reward groups receive separate value estimates and normalized advantages, then combine into one PPO update. This prevents dense regularization from overwhelming sparse task progress in this specific formulation.

The upward force is removable exploration scaffolding. It increases visits to lifted intermediate states early, then decreases after reaching head-height targets. A curriculum is successful only if the policy remains capable after the force reaches zero.

Action range also shrinks with competence. Early `β` allows broad discovery; later reduction to 0.25 forces smaller corrections. L2C2 controls local policy/value sensitivity while second-order action difference controls temporal jerk. They address different smoothness mechanisms.

## Method: input → processing → output

The actor observes base angular velocity, roll/pitch, joint position/velocity, previous action, the current action scale, and five frames of history. It outputs 23 relative joint-position increments. `pᵈ_t = p_t + βa_t` creates PD targets and `τ_t = K_p(pᵈ_t-p_t)-K_d ṗ_t` produces torque.

Base height divides behavior into righting, lifting, and final standing. Four critics model task, style, regularization, and post-stand objective groups; normalized advantages are weighted for PPO. When the trunk is sufficiently upright, a vertical force helps exploration, decreasing from 200 N in 20 N steps after progress. Action scale decreases from one to 0.25. Training uses ground, platform, wall, and slope plus random mass/CoM, friction, gains, torque disturbances, delay, and initial pose.

Training and deployment are asymmetric: critics and assistance exist only in training. The hardware path retains actor, action scaling, joint targets, and PD.

## Strongest experiment

Tables III and IV together are strongest. Repeated simulation ablations identify mechanisms, and hardware trials preserve a corresponding success/smoothness difference. They support the joint usefulness of critics, exploration scaffolding, and motion constraints for this setup—not a universal 20/20 safety guarantee.

The load and torque-loss negatives are particularly important. They show that degradation can be discontinuous. Reproduction should avoid converting 12 kg or a loss ratio into an operational limit without larger controlled testing and hardware-specific safety review.

## Paper-to-code mapping

- `HostGround.step` and `HostGround.update_force_curriculum` apply the vertical pull and reduce pull/action scale according to progress.
- `ActorCritic.critics` and `RolloutStorage.multi_critic_advantages` implement reward-group critics and advantage combination.
- `PPO.update`, `HostGround._reward_smoothness`, and `HostGround._compute_torques` implement L2C2-style smoothing, second-order action terms, scaling, and PD targets.
- `eval_ground.py` and related environment scripts evaluate success, foot movement, smoothness, and energy without training assistance.

The [pinned official repository](https://github.com/OpenRobotLab/HoST/tree/70bb580949a336a920833700e4b5dc3bf7fe87ce) is MIT licensed and contains training/simulation evaluation code, not a complete safety-certified hardware deployment package.

## Limitations and safety boundary

Author-stated limitations include collision during outdoor proprioceptive get-up, interference between supine and prone joint training, incomplete integration into a full humanoid system, hardware joint-speed mismatch, and unresolved H1/H1-2 transfer differences.

Independent limits are implicit contact without clearance sensing, 20 primary hardware trials, three-trial load/fault cells, and the risk that faster get-up optimizes impact or wear. Success must be reported with time, smoothness, slip, impulse, peak torque/speed, and safety termination.

Pull force, `β`, gains, payload, slopes, and paper fault settings are not transferable limits. Get-up requires fixed-seed simulation, collision review, supported low-speed posture tests, current/thermal protection, soft landing area, anti-pinch separation, emergency stop, and a dedicated safety operator.

## Bounded engineering takeaway

A demonstration-free policy still embeds strong human structure through height phases, reward groups, pull triggers, action schedules, and terrain design. Ablate multi-critic, removable force, action scaling, L2C2, and second-order action smoothing independently, while verifying assistance reaches zero before final evaluation.

## Reproduction and acceptance checklist

Log every critic's return, value loss, normalized advantage, and combined advantage by environment. Track pull force, head-height attainment, `β`, success, slip, smoothness, and energy through the curriculum. Evaluate a posture-environment matrix—supine, prone, side; ground, wall, platform, slope—with full denominators, contact sequence, head/hand collision, foot motion, peak velocity, and stop cause. Audit simulator termination and action clipping separately from communication timeout, current/temperature protection, collision detection, and emergency stop added for hardware.
